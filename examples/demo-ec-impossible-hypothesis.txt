Empirical question:
Does mandatory disclosure of restaurant hygiene grades raise average hygiene quality?

Baseline result:
After a city introduced mandatory posting of letter grades in restaurant windows, average
inspection scores rose by 4.2 points on a 100-point scale (event study around the mandate
date, Table 3, significant at 1%).

Proposed mechanism:
Disclosure makes hygiene observable to consumers before they choose a restaurant. When
quality is unobservable, a restaurant captures none of the demand return to investing in
hygiene, so it invests only up to what inspection penalties compel. Making quality
observable gives the restaurant a demand-side return to hygiene investment, so investment
rises.

Mechanism test:
We interact the mandate with a measure of pre-period consumer search intensity in the
neighborhood (mobile-location data on how many restaurants a diner visits before choosing).
The effect is larger where search intensity is higher (Table 5).

Heterogeneity results:
The effect is larger for restaurants in the middle of the pre-period quality distribution
and near zero for restaurants at the top and at the bottom (Table 6).

Key hypotheses to rationalize:
H1: Mandatory disclosure raises every restaurant's hygiene investment.
H2: The effect is larger where consumers search more.
H3: The effect is largest for mid-quality restaurants.

Preferred theoretical tradition, if any:
A vertical product differentiation model with unobservable quality and a disclosure
mandate — something in the Shapiro / Milgrom quality-disclosure tradition.

Scope exclusions:
No entry or exit of restaurants. No consumer learning over time; treat the disclosure
as a one-shot change in the information structure. No inspector behavior; the grading
technology is exogenous and truthful.

Declared empirical domain:
Restaurants with pre-period inspection scores between 60 and 95. Consumer search intensity
between 1 and 6 restaurants considered per decision. All restaurants remain active
throughout the sample.

Note for the pipeline:
H1 as stated is a claim about EVERY restaurant. We expect the model to deliver it. If it
cannot, we want to know that before we write the theory section.
