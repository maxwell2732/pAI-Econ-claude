mode: empirical-companion

Empirical question:
Does a government-subsidized credit guarantee program raise investment among small and
medium-sized manufacturing firms?

Baseline result:
Firms in prefectures that entered the guarantee program raise their investment rate by
roughly 3.1 percentage points relative to firms in not-yet-treated prefectures (staggered
difference-in-differences, Table 2, significant at 1%). The pre-trend test is flat over four
pre-periods.

Proposed mechanism:
The program lowers the collateral wedge firms face when borrowing. A firm that must post
collateral to borrow can only finance investment up to a multiple of its pledgeable assets.
The guarantee substitutes public credit risk for private collateral, relaxing that limit, so
firms that were previously borrowing-constrained can move closer to their unconstrained
optimum.

Mechanism test:
We interact the treatment indicator with a pre-period measure of the firm's collateral
ratio (tangible fixed assets over total assets). The treatment effect is significantly
larger for firms with low pre-period collateral ratios (Table 4, interaction coefficient
negative and significant at 5%). We also show that treated firms' loan-to-asset ratios rise
and that the increase is concentrated in medium-term loans.

Heterogeneity results:
The effect concentrates in firms with high pre-period leverage and low cash holdings —
firms most likely to have been constrained before the program. Firms in the top quartile of
cash holdings show no detectable effect (Table 5).

Key hypotheses to rationalize:
H1: The guarantee program raises the investment rate.
H2: The effect operates through the collateral constraint — it is larger where the
    collateral wedge binds harder.
H3: The effect is concentrated among firms that were financially constrained before the
    program, and is absent for unconstrained firms.

Preferred theoretical tradition, if any:
A static collateral-constrained investment model. We do not want a full dynamic
investment model.

Scope exclusions:
No general equilibrium effects on the interest rate. No entry or exit. No welfare analysis
of the subsidy — the paper does not claim the program is welfare-improving. No banking
sector behavior; treat the lender as passive.

Declared empirical domain:
All firms in the sample are borrowers with strictly positive debt. Collateral ratios lie
between 0.15 and 0.70. The guarantee covers at most 80% of loan principal. Investment is
strictly positive for all firms in the estimation sample (no corner at zero investment).
